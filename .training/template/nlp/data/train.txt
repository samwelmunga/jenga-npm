Natural language processing is a subfield of artificial intelligence.
Machine learning models learn patterns from data.
Deep learning uses neural networks with many layers.
Text classification assigns predefined categories to documents.
Tokenization splits raw text into individual words or subwords.
Word embeddings represent words as dense numerical vectors.
Sentiment analysis determines the emotional tone of a piece of text.
Named entity recognition identifies and classifies entities in text.
Part-of-speech tagging assigns grammatical roles to each word.
The transformer architecture revolutionized natural language processing tasks.
Transfer learning allows models trained on one task to generalize to others.
A corpus is a large collection of text used for training language models.
Stop words are common words that carry little semantic meaning.
Stemming reduces a word to its base or root form.
Lemmatization normalizes words to their dictionary form considering context.
Bag of words represents text as an unordered set of word counts.
TF-IDF measures the importance of a word in a document relative to a corpus.
Recurrent neural networks process sequential data like sentences.
Attention mechanisms help models focus on relevant parts of the input.
Byte pair encoding is a subword tokenization algorithm widely used today.
Language models assign probabilities to sequences of words.
Fine-tuning adapts a pre-trained model to a specific downstream task.
Data augmentation artificially increases training set size through transformations.
Cross-entropy loss is commonly used for classification tasks in NLP.
